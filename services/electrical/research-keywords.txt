Is there a difference between an electrician and an electrical contractor?
AI Overview
An electrician is a licensed tradesperson who performs hands-on installation and repair of electrical systems. An electrical contractor, on the other hand, is a business owner or licensed firm that manages entire projects, pulls permits, and hires teams of electricians to execute the work. 

Empowered Global, Inc.
 +1
Key Differences Breakdown
Scope of Work: Electricians focus on the technical execution—like wiring a home, installing fixtures, or troubleshooting faults. Electrical contractors handle the business and logistics, including comprehensive quotes, project management, and bringing building designs to life. 

Empowered Global, Inc.
 +1
Project Oversight: Electricians work independently or as part of a team. Contractors act as the project managers, taking responsibility for budgets, scheduling, and ensuring the whole job complies with local and national codes. 

Buell Electric
 +1
Licensing: While both are licensed, an electrical contractor typically holds a higher-level master electrician's license plus business certifications, giving them the legal authority to take out building permits and hire employees. Electricians (such as journeymen) perform the work under the contractor's permit.

 +2
When to Hire: Call an electrician directly for smaller, straightforward jobs like fixing a broken outlet, upgrading a light fixture, or adding a ceiling fan. Hire an electrical contractor for large-scale projects like new construction builds, commercial build-outs, or extensive home renovations that require multiple phases and workers.
